Your report shows a chart, not just a grade. A dot sits somewhere on two axes: Presentation on one, Qualifications on the other. Where that dot lands points you to one of four recommended moves. Here is what each axis is actually measuring, so the chart feels earned instead of mysterious.

## Two different questions

Presentation is about the document itself. It is a deterministic score built from five categories: parseability, whether the document extracts and parses cleanly; structure, whether it flows the way a reader expects; evidence, whether claims are backed by specifics instead of vague duties; positioning, whether the strongest material leads; and credibility, whether the document holds together and reads as trustworthy. Presentation does not know or care what job you want next. It is answering the same question no matter which target you point at.

Qualifications is a different question: how ready does your background look for a specific target role. It reads the substance behind the document, the roles, the scope, the trajectory, against what that target typically asks for. This is a readiness and competitiveness read, not a prediction. It is not telling you your odds of getting hired, it is not promising an interview, and it is not a live scan of the job market. It is an honest look at how your experience stacks up for the role you are aiming at, so you know whether the gap you need to close is on the page or in the background.

## The target is a variable, not a fact

Qualifications only means something once you know what it is being measured against. RezScore infers your target role from the resume itself and shows that guess next to your score, so you can correct it in one tap if it is wrong. Until a target is set, the read uses "your target role" in general terms.

This matters because the same resume aims differently at different targets. A background that reads as strong for one role can read as still developing for another, right next to it on the same page. Change the target and the Qualifications read changes with it. That's the chart doing its job: telling you how ready you are for the specific job you're chasing, instead of handing you one fixed verdict about you as a person.

## Apply to jobs

Both scores hold up. The document reads well, and the background backs it up for this target. There is not much editing left that would meaningfully move either number. The next move is real applications, tailored per posting rather than one resume sent everywhere.

## Polish your resume

Qualifications is strong, Presentation is behind it. Your background is ready for this target, the document just is not making the case for it yet. This is usually the most fixable spot on the chart. Fix the presentation problems, structure, evidence, positioning, before you send this version to anyone.

## Build your skills

Presentation is strong, Qualifications is behind it. The document reads well, but the substance behind it is still developing for this specific target. No amount of further polish closes that kind of gap, because the problem is not on the page. From here you have two honest paths: build more evidence toward this target, or consider whether a nearer target fits where you actually stand today.

## Get started

Both are still developing. That is not a dead end, it just means there is real work on both sides. The order matters: presentation first, because it is the faster fix, then put the freed-up effort into building the experience this target calls for.
